## Overview

Wheelchair toilet door sign

This is the third sign in a small set I designed for shared spaces, offices, and workshops where the restrooms only ever seem to get a handwritten note taped to the door, if anything at all. Clear, consistent signage for the accessible restroom matters just as much as, if not more than, the other two, and it is exactly the kind of small, practical object 3D printing is good for: a flat, high-contrast pictogram that would normally cost real money from a signage shop, made instead from a few grams of filament and a short print.

I kept the same visual language across all three signs in the set, a simple, high-contrast silhouette rather than a detailed illustration, so that all three read clearly at a glance and are instantly recognizable as belonging together on the same wall or hallway. The flat profile prints quickly with no supports, and comes off the bed as a rigid, wipeable sign that will hold up far longer than a printed paper sheet.

- **Height**: 20 cm
- **Thickness**: 3 mm

Use double-sided tape to hang it. The 3 mm thickness keeps the sign flat and rigid on the door without adding unnecessary weight, and the flat back sits cleanly against a painted or laminate door surface.

I print all three signs in the set from the same profile and filament color so they read as a matching set on the wall, even when printed at different times as replacements. If you only need this one right now, it prints just as cleanly on its own, and the other two are ready whenever you need them.

Clear, dedicated signage for the accessible restroom is not optional the way a decorative sign might be, it is a basic courtesy that costs almost nothing to provide once you already have a printer on hand.

Print all three once and you likely will not need to think about restroom signage again for a long time.

A small detail, but the kind that makes a shared space feel cared for.
